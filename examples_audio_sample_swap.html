Sample swap

Samples at addresses the game chooses: a resident set, and a swap area whose samples are replaced during play. audioLoadSampleAt() and audioLoadSampleBeginAt() place a sample, whole or in steps, where the game says, refuse a place that is not free, and leave audioLoadSample() working beside them. There is nothing to press: the phases run on their own and the backdrop colour names the phase (blue placing, red refusals, green replacement, yellow silence cases, purple timing, grey done).

What it shows

  1. Placing: two samples at $2000 and $3000; an ordinary audioLoadSample() after them goes above them ($3048); one placed just below the first (touching it) leaves the allocator where it was, and unloading it gives nothing back.
  2. Refusals, each with its code and nothing changed: slot 64, below $0B00, at the end of the area, past $FFFF, one byte on another sample, size 0, size under 3 in steps, on the load in steps, the slot being loaded, a second load in steps.
  3. Replacing during play: voice 0 holds the sample at $2000 while the one at $3000 is replaced in three steps of 24 bytes, then played.
  4. Silence before overwriting: the voice playing the replaced sample is left on, stopped just before the first step, stopped late in a frame, stopped one or two frames before. The ROM times each gap and the release.
  5. A note on another voice during a load in steps: twelve notes, one a frame, alone, after a 192-byte step and before one.

The samples are BRR written by hand in data.asm, block by block, so the manifests can compare their bytes in the sound CPU's memory. testing/manifests/audio_sample_swap.toml checks the places, the refusals, the bytes at each boundary and the notes' timing; audio_sample_swap_silence.toml checks the gaps against the release. Measured: the release takes 133 scanlines from audioStopVoice() (8 ms); a key-off just before the first step is not enough, two VBlanks are (see the tutorial).

SNES Concepts

  • The sample directory: two 16-bit addresses per sample, on any byte
  • A swap area of fixed places in the sound CPU's memory, filled while voices play
  • Key-off is a release, not a cut: the voice reads its sample until the envelope is down
  • BRR blocks: a header and sixteen 4-bit samples in 9 bytes

cd examples/audio/sample_swap && make
