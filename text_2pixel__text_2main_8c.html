Detailed Description

Text — proportional text composed at the pixel in a 2bpp layer.

The text module puts one glyph per tile, on the tile grid. A game that wants a narrow font at a 6-pixel pitch, or a label at any x and y, composes its text itself: a 2bpp tile layer kept in RAM, each glyph ORed into it at a pixel offset, the layer sent to VRAM when it changes. The primitive is tileBlitGlyph2bpp(): it puts rows of a glyph into one tile and the tile to its right, shifted by 0 to 7 pixels. A glyph placed at (x, y) touches up to four tiles — the row of tiles holding y and the one below — so two calls cover it, one per tile row. Nothing here is an asset: a 5x7 font is declared as bits and turned into 2bpp glyphs at boot.

SNES Concepts
  • A 2bpp tile is 16 bytes, planes 0 and 1 interleaved per row; a layer of N tiles is N x 16 bytes, and tile n of the layer is tile n of the sheet
  • The tilemap only places tiles: the pixels come from the layer, so text at any position is a matter of writing the right bytes of the right tiles
  • A glyph at x covers tile x / 8 and, shifted by x % 8, the tile after it; at y it covers rows y % 8 to 7 of one tile row and 0 to y % 8 - 1 of the next
  • The layer lives in FAR RAM (2.5 KB) and is cleared with memset() before a new layout; the upload is split over two VBlanks (1280 bytes each)
What to Observe
  • Four lines of white text at a 6-pixel pitch, none aligned on the tile grid (y = 5, 21, 38, 51)
  • Press A: every line moves one pixel to the right (six steps, then back), the glyphs crossing tile boundaries as they go
