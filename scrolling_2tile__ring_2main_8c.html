Detailed Description

Eight-direction scrolling over a map of 1,433 distinct characters.

An archipelago of 1280x896 pixels, 160x112 characters, drawn with 1,433 distinct 8x8 characters once mirrored copies are merged: more than the 1,024 a tilemap entry can name, so the whole set never fits VRAM. The screen never shows more than 389 at once (the converter measured every position), and the tilering module keeps a ring of 640 character slots in VRAM: as the camera moves it loads the characters of the column and the row that come into view, and frees the slots of those that leave.

The camera flies a fixed course on its own — up, across, down at a diagonal, at 3 to 7 pixels a frame — so that a test sees the same frames every time; the D-pad takes over (B held: fast), START gives the course back. The figures the course produced are kept in tr_* variables for the test (testing/manifests/scrolling_tile_ring*.toml, testing/tile_ring_budget.py).

The map is invented: devtools/art_assets/tile_ring.py draws it from a fixed seed.

SNES Concepts
  • A tilemap entry names 1,024 characters: a large map needs streaming
  • A ring of character slots, freed by reference count, refilled in sequence
  • A 64x32 tilemap as a window that follows the camera on both axes
  • VRAM transfers noted during the frame, sent in VBlank (vramqueue)
  • The VBlank budget: what one frame of scrolling costs, NTSC and PAL
What to Observe
  • The map scrolls in all eight directions without a wrong character
  • Nothing to see of the ring itself: that is the point

console, background, input, tilering, vramqueue
