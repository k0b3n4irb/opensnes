Detailed Description

Super FX game skeleton — a game loop at 60 fps while the GSU renders.

The shape of a Super FX game: the CPU runs the game every frame (here, a sight steered with the D-pad, and music), the GSU renders the 3D view on its own clock (here, a hologram turning above a square of the city at night: a data cube, a smaller cube inside it, the struts between them and the projector's beams), and the NMI moves each finished frame to VRAM. Nothing waits for anything else.

  • The renderer runs from the GSU's code cache (gsuCacheLoad / gsuStartCached): the ROM stays the CPU's, so the game loop runs during the job.
  • Two framebuffers in Game Pak RAM, two char blocks in VRAM: gsuPresent() queues the frame the job just drew, the next job draws in the other buffer, and the NMI moves the queued one a piece per VBlank, taking Game Pak RAM from the GSU for each piece. BG1 switches to the new block only once all of it has landed.
  • A 40 + 40 line letterbox (gsuSetupHdmaBlanking) lengthens the blank the NMI transfers in: a 16 KB frame lands in two VBlanks, 30 fps.
SNES Concepts
  • Super FX code cache: a job that leaves the ROM to the CPU
  • Game Pak RAM time-sharing (SCMR RAN) between the GSU and the NMI's DMA
  • Double buffering by BG1 char base (BG12NBA), swapped after a whole frame
  • HDMA letterbox on INIDISP to extend the VBlank transfer window
  • A GSU bitmap BG over a picture BG: the GSU clears to colour 0, which is transparent, so BG2's square shows wherever the hologram is not
  • OBJ layer over both
  • SNESMOD music fed every frame (snesmodProcess) during GSU work
  • The CPU projects, the GSU draws: 19 points a job in Game Pak RAM, joined by the GSU through an edge table written once at boot
  • Cartridge RAM from C through FAR pointers ($70:8000)
What to Observe
  • The hologram turns and bobs at 30 fps above the projector in the middle of the square; the sight follows the D-pad at 60 fps, whatever the GSU is doing, and the music never stutters.
  • No torn or half-drawn hologram: a frame is shown only once complete.
