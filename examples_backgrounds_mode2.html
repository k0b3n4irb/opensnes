mode2 — offset-per-tile in Mode 2 and Mode 4: per-column scroll from BG3

Backgrounds · the offset-per-tile modes (2/4/6).

Why it matters for your game

Modes 2, 4 and 6 have a trick no other mode offers: offset-per-tile (OPT) lets every 8-pixel column of a layer scroll independently. That is how you get a flag rippling in the wind, a heat-haze shimmer, water distortion, or cheap per-column parallax — effects that would otherwise cost a scanline of HDMA or a full software redraw. It is niche, but when you want it, nothing else does it as cheaply.

Screenshot

What you'll learn

The single idea: in Mode 2, BG3 stops being a drawable layer and becomes an offset table. For each column of BG1/BG2, the PPU reads a scroll offset straight out of BG3's tilemap:

  • Per screen column N ≥ 1 (column 0 can't be offset), the PPU reads BG3 tile-row 0 for the horizontal offset word and tile-row 1 for the vertical offset word.
  • Offset word: bit 0x2000 = apply to BG1 (0x4000 = BG2); the V value is bits 0–9, the H value bits 3–9 (8-pixel-granular).

This example puts the Rue basse under the rain on BG1 (the 4bpp picture of mode1) and writes a small sine into BG3's V-offset row, so each column shows the street a few lines higher or lower: the air shimmers like the heat over a steam vent. The phase advances each frame.

  • A vertical word is the raw scroll value. It replaces the layer's VOFS for its column entirely (snesdev-wiki, Offset-per-tile: "Vertical entries will replace the BG VOFS value entirely"), so the -1 the lib adds to a vertical scroll (KNOWN_LIMITATIONS, "Vertical scroll is off by one") is not in it. A word of 0x3FF (-1) puts a column where column 0 is; the wave here goes from -1 to 2 lines. Modes 4 and 6 reuse this exact data path, and press B for Mode 4.

Mode 4: one row, one word per column (B)

Mode 4

Mode 4 gives you an 8bpp (256-colour) BG1, a 2bpp BG2, and — like Mode 2 — BG3 as the offset table. The difference is the table: Mode 4 reads one row of it, so each column gets one word, either vertical or horizontal:

  • The Mode 4 offset word: bit 15 = 1 vertical, 0 horizontal; bit 13 = apply to BG1 (bit 14 = BG2); the value in bits 0–9, of which a horizontal offset ignores the low 3 (snesdev-wiki, Offset-per-tile).
  • One row: BG3 row 0 holds the 32 words; word 0 applies to the second visible column — the leftmost column is never offset.
  • BG3VOFS picks the row: bgSetScroll(2, 0, 0) reads row 0. The lib writes BG3's VOFS raw in Modes 2/4/6 (for a displayed layer it writes y - 1; see KNOWN_LIMITATIONS, "Vertical scroll is off by one").

Here BG1 is the canal at night in 256 colours (the 8bpp picture of mode3, with eight mirrored rows added below it for the lines the wave lowers into view), and the same sine goes into the row as vertical words: the columns ripple. Press A and it goes in as horizontal words: the columns slide sideways, 0 to 24 pixels, 8 at a time. B again goes back to Mode 2.

The two pictures do not fit in VRAM together (28 KB of 4bpp tiles, 50 KB of 8bpp), so a switch reloads the scene in force blank (setScreenOff, the tiles, the map and the palette by DMA, setMode, setScreenOn): one dark frame. VRAM: the scene's tiles at $0000 (the canal's second part at $4000), BG1's map at $7000, the offset table at $7400.

What to observe / if it breaks

  • Correct run: the street shimmers as a travelling wave; each 8-pixel column is at its own vertical offset (look at the noodle sign's edges), all from one 32-entry BG3 table.
  • The street stays still: the offset table never reached VRAM. The classic cause is computing it after WaitForVBlank — the upload then runs past VBlank and the PPU drops it. Build the table during active display, DMA it the instant VBlank starts (what this example does).
  • Only the leftmost column is wrong: column 0 cannot be offset by OPT — that is hardware, not a bug.
  • Every column but the first sits one line too high: the words carry the lib's -1 convention twice, or not at all. They are raw values (above).
  • Whole layer shifts uniformly (no ripple): your offset words are missing the 0x2000 enable bit, so the PPU ignores them and uses the base scroll.

Probe oracle: wave_phase advances every frame. The luna manifests dma_mode2_opt_table.toml (Mode 2: the two rows, word by word, at a given loop iteration) and backgrounds_mode2_mode4.toml (B, then A: the vertical words, then the horizontal ones) check it.
