The APU

The audio processing unit: a sound CPU (SPC700), a sound generator (S-DSP) and their own 64 KB of RAM (ARAM), apart from the main CPU. This page is the hardware; music is Music with SNESMOD, samples from C and SPC700 programs of your own are Samples, Voices and the Raw APU, and the converters are opensnes-sample — WAV to BRR samples (WAV to BRR) and opensnes-music — Impulse Tracker to soundbank (Impulse Tracker to soundbank).

Overview

Component Details
SPC700 8-bit CPU at 1.024 MHz
ARAM 64 KB, shared by program, data, samples and the echo ring
S-DSP 8 voices playing BRR samples
Output 32 kHz stereo

What the S-DSP does:

  • 8 voices at once, each with its own volume, pitch and envelope (ADSR or GAIN)
  • BRR-compressed samples (16 samples in 9 bytes, about 3.5:1)
  • Echo with an 8-tap FIR filter, up to 240 ms of delay
  • Pitch modulation of a voice by the previous one
  • A noise generator shared by all voices

The main CPU cannot touch ARAM: it uploads a program and data through the IPL boot ROM, then talks to that program through four ports.

The sound driver, the samples and the echo ring share the 64 KB. The SDK's drivers and what they leave for samples: Music with SNESMOD and Samples, Voices and the Raw APU.

Communication ports

SNES side SPC700 side Purpose
$2140 $F4 Port 0
$2141 $F5 Port 1
$2142 $F6 Port 2
$2143 $F7 Port 3

Each side reads what the other wrote. Use 8-bit writes to the ports. The official manual (Book 1, §3-9-6) warns that a 16-bit write to $2140/$2141 can also write $2143; the mechanism is disputed on nesdev, so treat it as a caution rather than a measured fact — 8-bit writes are safe either way.

Upload through the IPL ROM

At reset the SPC700 runs its boot ROM, which takes blocks from the main CPU (anomie's SPC700 doc, IPL Boot ROM):

  1. Wait until $2140 reads $AA and $2141 reads $BB.
  2. Write the destination address to $2142-$2143, a non-zero byte to $2141, then $CC to $2140, and wait until $2140 reads $CC back.
  3. For each byte: write it to $2141, write its index (from $00) to $2140, and wait until $2140 echoes the index.
  4. Repeat for further blocks; to finish, send the entry point with $00 in $2141, and the SPC700 jumps to it.

The SDK's drivers do this for you; writing your own SPC700 program is the raw APU path of Samples, Voices and the Raw APU.

Pitch

The 14-bit pitch is a 2.12 fixed-point rate: $1000 plays the sample at 32 000 Hz, its own speed if it was recorded at 32 kHz. The rate is pitch × 32000 / $1000 Hz, up to $3FFF, almost two octaves above $1000 (SNESdev wiki, S-DSP registers, VxPITCH). One octave is a factor 2: a sample tuned to C at $1000 gives the A above it near $1AE9.

Noise

FLG bits 0-4 set the noise rate for every voice that has its NON bit: $00 stops it, $10 is 500 Hz, $1F is 32 kHz.

BRR samples

BRR (bit rate reduction) is an ADPCM format: blocks of 9 bytes, each holding 16 samples of 4 bits.

Byte 0: header
Bits 7-4: range (shift amount)
Bits 3-2: filter (0-3)
Bit 1: loop flag
Bit 0: end flag
Bytes 1-8: 16 samples of 4 bits
Filter a b Use
0 0 0 No prediction
1 0.9375 0 Simple prediction
2 1.90625 -0.9375 Strong prediction
3 1.796875 -0.8125 Moderate prediction

A loop point falls on a block boundary, so it is a multiple of 16 samples.

Sample directory

DIR gives the directory's page (×$100). Entry n (the SRCN of a voice) is 4 bytes:

Offset Content
+0 Sample start address (16-bit)
+2 Loop address (16-bit)

Envelopes

ADSR

With ADSR1 bit 7 set, the voice follows attack, decay, sustain, then release at key off:

ADSR1 (x5) EDDD AAAA
│└┴┴ └┴┴┴─ Attack rate (0-15)
│ └──── Decay rate (0-7)
└───────── ADSR enable
ADSR2 (x6) SSSR RRRR
│││ └┴┴┴┴─ Sustain rate (0-31)
└┴┴──────── Sustain level (0-7)

The rates are indices into a fixed table of the chip, not times. Attack rises linearly (rate 15 is the fastest), decay falls exponentially to the sustain level, sustain keeps falling exponentially at its own rate (0 holds the level), and release, after key off, falls linearly at a fixed speed of 8 per sample (fullsnes, DSP ADSR/Gain Envelope; SNESdev wiki, DSP envelopes).

GAIN

With ADSR off, GAIN drives the envelope:

Bits Mode
0vvv vvvv Direct: hold the envelope at v × 16
100r rrrr Linear decrease at rate r
101r rrrr Exponential decrease
110r rrrr Linear increase
111r rrrr Bent increase (fast, then slow near the top)

Echo

The echo ring takes EDL × 2 KB of ARAM from page ESA, and delays the sound by EDL × 16 ms: EDL 15 is 240 ms and 30 KB. The DSP writes the ring continuously while echo writing is on (FLG bit 5 clear), so anything else placed there is overwritten: plan ARAM around it.

Register Example value Effect
ESA $40 Ring at $4000
EDL $02 32 ms delay, 4 KB ring
EFB $60 Moderate feedback
EVOLL/EVOLR $40 Echo at half volume

The FIR filter's eight coefficients (COEF, $0F-$7F) shape the echo: 127, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0 passes it unchanged; 32, 32, 32, 32, 0, 0, 0, 0 is a low-pass.
