SFX voices

Effects driven voice by voice, the way a game's own sound player does it, and the cost of each step counted in scanlines. There is nothing to press: the phases run on their own and the backdrop colour names the phase (blue loading, green waveform sweep, yellow restarts, red seven voices, grey done).

What it shows

  1. A looped one-cycle waveform whose pitch changes every frame: the pitch you send with audioSetVoiceVolumePitch() is the note.
  2. Stop, then play, on the same voice inside one frame: audioStopVoice() then audioPlaySampleOn(), the "last sound to arrive cuts the one in progress" rule.
  3. A sample sent in pieces: audioLoadSampleBegin() and one audioLoadSampleStep() per frame, while the game runs.
  4. Seven voices updated every frame, and what that costs.

The oracles (load_lines_max, restart_lines_max, seven_lines_max, …) are globals; testing/manifests/audio_sfx_voices.toml reads them. Measured: a 192-byte step 115 scanlines at most, a stop+play pair 18, a pitch update 3, seven voices 29 (1 scanline = 1 364 master cycles).

SNES Concepts

  • A looped one-cycle waveform: pitch is the note
  • The game picks the voice; the module obeys
  • Piecewise upload over the APU ports: a bounded cost per frame
  • Counting a cost in scanlines from the code itself

How to Build

cd examples/audio/sfx_voices && make

res/wave.wav is written by gen_wave.py; the build converts it with opensnes-sample (no .brr is committed).

Modules Used

console, dma, audio, profile
