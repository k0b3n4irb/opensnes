rpg — an RPG template driven by a Tiled map
Screenshot

The SDK's modules composed into a playable RPG skeleton. Kit the courier walks the Rue basse at night, between a noodle bar, a garage, a junk shop and a canal; the residents each have a line, a locker holds ten credits, and the teal door of an apartment block leads into the raccoon's den. Three things make it a template rather than a demo:

1. The map is a real Tiled map. res/town.tmj holds the terrain, the per-tile collision (each tile's attribute property), the entity positions (the Entities object layer: spawn, residents, the lockers, the door) and each resident's line and look (text and look properties on the object). Adding a resident is a map edit, not a code edit. The build converts the map with the SDK's opensnes-level (res/town.tmj.toml): the VRAM tilemap, one collision byte per cell, and the entities as C defines.

2. A real bordered dialog box, and a HUD. Both are 9-slice panels on BG2 with their text on BG3 above — the classic SNES RPG window, with the layers stacking scene < panel < text. The HUD carries hearts and credits; the credits go up by 10 when you open the lockers.

3. Two scenes. The street, and the den. A scene is a tileset + a palette + a tilemap + a collision map + entities, and the den is its own Tiled map (res/house.tmj). Walking into the door swaps them; the raccoon greets you with no button press.

Input Action
D-pad walk (one metatile, 16 px, per step)
A talk to a resident (face it) / open the lockers (face them)
walk into the teal door enter the den; the mat inside takes you back

ROM mode: LoROM (project default).

SNES Concepts

  • Tiled as the content pipeline: collision and entities are data. opensnes-level reads town.tmj and writes street.q16 (the 64×64 tilemap in the PPU's four 32×32 pages), street.c16 (a collision byte per cell, what collideTile() reads) and town_entities.inc. The residents' table, NPC_TABLE, takes its fields from the objects' properties: u8 tx; u8 ty; const char *text; u16 look;.
  • Metatile movement: the map is 8×8 cells, the world is walked on a grid of 16×16 metatiles. A character occupies one metatile; its 32×32 sprite is drawn over it, feet on it and head overhanging upward (8 px left and 16 px up of the metatile) — the standard top-down convention. A step asks collideTile() about the metatile's four cells, and is refused where a resident stands.
  • A 32×32 sprite is a 4×4 block of the OBJ name table, which is 16 tiles wide. Kit's walk is one sheet of 15 frames, four to a 128-px row, so it goes to VRAM in one DMA; each resident is a frame converted on its own, which the converter writes as four rows of 16 tiles with the frame in the first four of each, so load_resident() copies it in four DMAs of 128 bytes, one name-table row apart. The residents live in the second name table (tiles 256 and up, word $7000).
  • The plan owns the sprite-palette slots: res/palettes.toml lists the OBJ palettes, one per character; the build runs opensnes-palette plan (see docs/tools/opensnes-palette.md) to assign each a collision-free CGRAM slot and generate res/palettes.inc. main.c uses PAL_OWL_CGRAM / PAL_OWL_SLOT and so on, so adding a resident never desyncs the code from the layout — no hand-counted 128 / 144. The BG palettes stay hand-managed on purpose: the two scenes swap the same slot 0 and the text layer owns slot 1 at runtime, a dynamic layout a static plan does not model.
  • Off-camera entities must be hidden, not just drawn: OAM X is 9 bits and Y is 8, so an entity outside the camera does not vanish — its coordinates wrap and it reappears somewhere plausible, e.g. a resident standing inside a wall. draw_char() parks anything off-camera with oamHide().
  • Forced blank, not brightness 0, around a big VRAM upload: setScreenOff() sets INIDISP bit 7, the only state besides VBlank in which the PPU accepts VRAM writes. setBrightness(0) just makes the screen black — the PPU keeps fetching and the write is dropped. A scene switch moves 37 KB (the street's 939 tiles, its 8 KB map, a palette).
  • A fixed 16-colour palette per scene, imposed on the converter (palette = "town_fixed.pal" in res/tileset.png.toml) instead of quantised from the picture. With a quantiser, adding one tile re-derives the whole palette and every existing tile shifts hue. Both scenes are drawn in the city's 15 colours, so the two fixed palettes are the same.
  • Two 9-slice panels on one layer via the panel module: the HUD at the top and the dialog box at the bottom share BG2's tilemap, so one upload covers both and opening a dialog never disturbs the HUD. BG2 shares BG3's character base: an empty cell of a panel is tile 0, so the first 16 words there are kept zero and the font starts after them. panelPut() takes an absolute tile, the sheet's index plus its base.
  • Collision through the SDK's collideTile() over the converted map. Its tilemap parameter is const, so the lookup is a bank-honouring far read — the 4 KB maps need neither bank $00 nor a byte of RAM.

VRAM Layout

Word address Content
$0000 the scene's tiles (939 for the street, to $3AB0; 470 for the den)
$4000 BG1's map, 64×64
$5000 BG2's and BG3's character base: a blank tile, then the font
$5400 BG2's tiles: the frame and the HUD icons (tile 64 from the base)
$5800 BG3's map (the text)
$5C00 BG2's map (the panels)
$6000 Kit's walk (OBJ tiles 0-239)
$7000 the residents (OBJ tiles 256 and up)

Editing the map

Open res/town.tmj in Tiled: paint terrain with the tileset's tiles, drag the Entities objects, edit a resident's text or look, then make. Tile collision is the attribute property on each tileset tile (FF00 = solid, 0 = walkable). Entities are objects in the Entities layer, typed spawn, npc, locker or door (the den's are arrival, host and mat). A resident's look indexes looks[] in main.c: 0 the owl, 1 the frog, 2 the rat, 3 the pangolin, 4 the raccoon.

The street itself is composed by devtools/art_assets/rpg.py (contributors). The ground is laid on the metatile grid from PixelLab's tiles (assets/neo-lumen/tiles/): two Wang sets, road to sidewalk and canal to sidewalk, placed by the terrain at each metatile's corners, so the curbs and the quays follow the layout; variants of the full tiles (cracks, a puddle, a grate) drawn from a seeded generator so every build is the same; the street's centre line, the bridge deck with its railings, the alleys. The buildings and props of assets/neo-lumen/town/ are placed over it, and the whole is cut into its distinct 8×8 tiles — 32 to a row of the tileset, the width the tileset converter's map is written in. Running it again rewrites both maps from its layout.
